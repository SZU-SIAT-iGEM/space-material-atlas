Orbit-AL — EPFL 2026
The problem. Every satellite in low Earth orbit eventually re-enters and burns up by design, and the number of objects re-entering grows with every new constellation. Ablation releases metals, mainly aluminium and iron, into the stratosphere, where they are thought to end up in the sulfuric acid aerosol layer. These metals have been hypothesised to activate chlorine on particle surfaces, absorb sunlight, seed polar stratospheric clouds and, once deposited, fertilise or stress ocean ecosystems. None of this has been measured on real re-entry material: models and laboratory studies rely on stand-ins such as pure α-Al₂O₃ and commercial powders, because no one knows what chemical form these metals actually take.
What we target. Stratospheric aerosol particles containing spacecraft-derived aluminium and iron, sampled in situ between the ground and 30 km. Re-entry origin is attributed through tracer elements (Li, Cu, Pb, Nb, Hf) reported in aircraft measurements.
The system. A balloon-borne sampler, RoDS, draws air through a dry PTFE filter under a 2 kg payload constraint. Collected particles undergo two extractions of different strength, a mild ultrapure-water leach and a strong reducing acid leach, and each extract is analysed twice: by ICP-MS for elemental composition and binding strength, and by engineered E. coli biosensors for what a living cell can actually access. The iron sensor reads intracellular Fe²⁺ through a Fur-regulated promoter, with ΔfeoB and ΔtonB knockouts used to recover the oxidation state from the uptake route; the aluminium sensor pairs a plant magnesium transporter with a computationally designed aluminium-binding element and a split-GFP readout.
The output. Not a material, but a set of measured, operationally defined numbers: the surface-accessible versus total metal fraction, the Fe(II)/Fe(III) ratio, and the bioavailable fraction, together with the extraction protocols needed to reproduce them.
Role in a future space system. Orbit-AL sits at the monitoring end of the material chain. Its values are meant to replace assumptions in atmospheric models, to give experimentalists a target when preparing analogue particles, and to test whether chemical leaching is a valid proxy for biological uptake of re-entry iron. As constellations scale, this is the kind of ground-truth data that end-of-life policy and environmental impact assessment will need.
